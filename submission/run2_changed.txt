│  Quantum computing is frequently hyped as a paradigm shift, but a rigorous analysis reveals significant engineering bottlenecks.        │
│  While qubits leverage superposition and entanglement to represent exponential state spaces (2ⁿ configurations), current hardware is   │
│  severely constrained by environmental noise. Today's Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) processors experience error rates between│
│  0.1% and 1%, limiting computations to a few hundred gate operations before decoherence destroys the signal.                             │
│                                                                                                                                         │
│  High-profile claims like Google's 2019 Sycamore experiment demonstrated quantum advantage only on synthetic, non-useful random-circuit │
│  sampling tasks, and classical algorithm improvements have since narrowed that gap. Furthermore, fault-tolerant quantum computing     │
│  remains distant: surface-code error correction requires an estimated 10,000 to 100,000 physical qubits to yield a single logical qubit│
│  with acceptable error rates (<10⁻⁹). Near-term practical utility is restricted to narrow domains like VQE or small-molecule chemistry. │
│  Without scalable error-corrected architectures, commercial quantum computing claims must be viewed with heavy technical skepticism.    │
